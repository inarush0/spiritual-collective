# Seed framework inclusion and attribution audit

## Research question

Which parts of the authorized four-pathway seed framework and its practice definitions should be corrected, attributed, reframed, qualified, consolidated, or removed before they are used in an inclusive, non-proselytizing resource for pediatric hospice and palliative-care patients and families?

## Scope and method

This audit reviewed the two authorized seed PDFs in place; they were not copied into the repository. The recommendations below compare that material with first-party guidance from palliative-care, pediatric, public-health, trauma-care, religious-studies, and tradition-owning organizations. This is a content audit, not clinical clearance of a finished practice library. Every released practice still needs the planned chaplain review, and practices tied to a living religious tradition need a qualified reviewer from that tradition.

## Answer in brief

The four pathways can remain as a gentle, optional navigation device, but not as a validated typology, assessment, or set of mutually exclusive spiritual identities. The individual practices need substantial editorial work before publication. The largest changes are to:

1. remove the framework's default assumption that every visitor relates to "the Divine";
2. replace directive and destiny-oriented language ("called," "should," "lead you closer") with invitations and an explicit choice to decline;
3. identify the traditions that own or particularly shape named practices instead of placing them in a generic spiritual pool;
4. separate practices that the seed combines even though they have different traditions, meanings, and instructions;
5. remove unsupported causal promises, especially around manifesting and affirmations; and
6. put fasting, essential oils, movement, breath practices, and contact with animals behind prominent pediatric safety constraints.

The National Consensus Project's palliative-care guideline makes the governing standard clear: spirituality can include meaning, purpose, relationship, and the significant or sacred; support must respect age, development, culture, tradition, and preference; patients and families may decline spiritual support; and practitioners must never impose their beliefs.[Clinical Practice Guidelines for Quality Palliative Care, Domain 5](https://www.nationalcoalitionhpc.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/NCHPC-NCPGuidelines_4thED_web_FINAL.pdf) The American Academy of Pediatrics likewise says children should participate according to their preferences, cultural and spiritual tradition, illness experience, developmental capacity, and consciousness.[Pediatric Palliative Care and Hospice Care Commitments, Guidelines, and Recommendations](https://publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article/132/5/966/31744/Pediatric-Palliative-Care-and-Hospice-Care)

## Framework-level disposition

### Retain, but explicitly qualify: the four pathways

Retain **Contemplative**, **Devotional**, **Embodied**, and **Service-oriented** as overlapping browse labels. Describe them as one editorial way to organize possibilities, not four universal types of spirituality. Do not score, diagnose, or tell a visitor which type they are. A practice may have several pathway tags, and a visitor may choose several, choose none, or go directly to all practices.

This treatment matches the seed's useful statement that categories overlap, while avoiding a new claim that the four-part taxonomy is established or comprehensive. No provenance for a validated four-path model was supplied with the authorized material. The site should therefore claim only that this is its navigation model.

Replace "What type of spiritual practice are you drawn to today?" with a lower-pressure prompt such as "What might support you right now?" Include "I'm not sure," "Show me everything," and "Nothing right now." This follows palliative-care guidance to respect refusal and distinct patient/family preferences, as well as trauma-informed principles of safety, collaboration, empowerment, voice, and choice.[NCP Domain 5](https://www.nationalcoalitionhpc.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/NCHPC-NCPGuidelines_4thED_web_FINAL.pdf) [SAMHSA, Trauma-Informed Approaches and Programs](https://www.samhsa.gov/mental-health/trauma-violence/trauma-informed-approaches-programs)

### Reframe: spirituality and outcome language

Use an inclusive umbrella such as "what feels meaningful, connecting, sacred, or spiritually important to you." Offer God/Divine wording only inside content a visitor selects as compatible with their beliefs. Do not use "the Divine" in global navigation or promise that every practice leads closer to it. Spiritual needs can differ even within one family, and a visitor may be nonreligious, uncertain, angry at God, spiritually harmed, or uninterested in spiritual care.[NCP Domain 5](https://www.nationalcoalitionhpc.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/NCHPC-NCPGuidelines_4thED_web_FINAL.pdf)

Do not imply that calm, hope, gratitude, compassion, faith, or acceptance is the correct outcome. Practices should permit grief, anger, fear, numbness, doubt, lament, and stopping. SAMHSA's trauma-informed model prioritizes psychological safety and resisting retraumatization rather than directing a person toward a prescribed emotional state.[SAMHSA's Concept of Trauma and Guidance for a Trauma-Informed Approach](https://library.samhsa.gov/sites/default/files/sma14-4884.pdf)

### Add: pathways and states the seed underrepresents

The navigation should not suggest that spiritual life consists only of stillness, devotion, body/creativity, or helping. Tag or otherwise make findable:

- **connection and belonging**, including receiving care and being accompanied;
- **grief, lament, doubt, anger, and spiritual struggle**;
- **meaning, memory, legacy, and relationship**;
- **rest and simply being**, without a task or improvement goal; and
- **tradition-specific support**, including contacting one's own faith/community leader or a chaplain.

These do not necessarily require a fifth pathway; they can be needs, moods, or goals that cut across all four. WHO defines palliative care around physical, psychological, social, and spiritual suffering, while the AAP calls for collaborative support of children and families across those realms.[WHO, Palliative care](https://www.who.int/health-topics/palliative-care) [AAP pediatric palliative-care statement](https://publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article/132/5/966/31744/Pediatric-Palliative-Care-and-Hospice-Care)

## Practice-level disposition

### Correct and consolidate

| Seed element | Disposition before publication |
| --- | --- |
| "Mid-Body Practices" | Correct to **mind-body practices**. Keep this as a broad accessibility tag, not as a tradition. |
| "Aroma Therapy" | Correct to **aromatherapy**, but apply the safety restrictions below. |
| Meditation / yoga / mind-body practices | Split into separate records. Meditation is a broad family; yoga is a specific ancient Indian practice with poses, meditation, controlled breathing, chanting, and other techniques, not a synonym for generic stretching or meditation.[UNESCO, Yoga](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/Yoga-01163) |
| Prayer beads / Rosary | Split. **Rosary** should be presented as a specifically Christian, especially Roman Catholic, form; **prayer beads** is a broader object category whose prayers and meanings vary by tradition. Do not give one generic instruction for both. |
| Mantras / chanting | Split or label as a broad family with tradition-specific variants. Do not detach a sacred mantra from its language and tradition or suggest that all chanting is mantra practice. |
| Guided visualization / imagery | Consolidate the duplicate entries under one neutral term, with an opt-out and grounding alternative. |
| Animals | Rename according to the actual action: for example, "spending time with a companion animal" or "noticing animals." It is not itself a practice instruction. Apply infection and consent constraints below. |
| Hobbies / art / music / play / journaling | Retain as broad, nonexclusive practices. Describe how a person may use one for expression, comfort, connection, prayer, or meaning without declaring the activity inherently spiritual. |
| Blessings | Add a definition or remove from the tree. It appears in the tree but not the definitions guide. Distinguish receiving, offering, and composing a blessing. |
| Prayer and worship | Add definitions or replace these vague umbrella entries with specific, optional practices. Do not assume a visitor's tradition permits self-led rites. |
| Using gifts and hobbies to uplift others | Consolidate under small acts of service, while also validating receiving care and conserving energy. |

### Attribute and contextualize

Use tradition labels as invitations (for example, "From Christian contemplative traditions") and include internal diversity rather than speaking for all members of a religion. The American Academy of Religion's official religious-literacy guidelines call for accurate knowledge, recognition of internal diversity, historical and cultural context, and a distinction between a tradition's own prescriptive claims and neutral description.[AAR Religious Literacy Guidelines](https://aarweb.org/resource/aar-religious-literacy-guidelines-2/)

At minimum:

- **Centering prayer, contemplative prayer, Jesus Prayer, Lectio Divina, Christian scripture study, the Christian liturgical year, and the Rosary** need explicit Christian attribution, with the narrower tradition named where the instructions are narrower. The Jesus Prayer wording should be selectable Christian content, never a generic repetition exercise. The Church of England itself presents the Jesus Prayer as a Christian prayer.[Church of England, Learning to pray](https://www.churchofengland.org/prayer-and-worship/learning-pray) The Holy See describes the Rosary as contemplative, Christ-centered prayer rather than generic use of beads.[Rosarium Virginis Mariae](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/apost_letters/2002/documents/hf_jp-ii_apl_20021016_rosarium-virginis-mariae.html)
- **Book of Common Prayer** needs Anglican attribution and edition/context. It is a source of worship and doctrine in the Church of England, not a generic devotional anthology.[Church of England, The Book of Common Prayer](https://www.churchofengland.org/prayer-and-worship/worship-texts-and-resources/book-common-prayer)
- **Prayer wheels** need Tibetan Buddhist attribution and tradition-reviewed instructions. A Tibetan Buddhist organization describes them as sacred objects containing mantras, not as interchangeable with prayer beads or generic calming props.[Foundation for the Preservation of the Mahayana Tradition, Resources on Holy Objects](https://fpmt.org/education/teachings/holy-objects/)
- **Yoga and chakras** need Indian-origin and tradition-specific context. Yoga must not be reduced to exercise; UNESCO identifies it as an ancient Indian practice with physical, mental, and spiritual dimensions.[UNESCO, Yoga](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/Yoga-01163) "Chakras" should be described as concepts within particular Indian religious and philosophical traditions, not as anatomically established energy centers or a breath technique.
- **Mantras, altars, shrines, amulets, charms, fasting, sacred walks, and chanting** appear across many traditions. Each published exercise must name the particular tradition and source it actually follows, or remain a clearly secular, user-defined activity without borrowing sacred forms. Avoid singular claims such as "Tradition X believes..." and have the relevant tradition reviewer approve it.
- **Labyrinth** should not be assigned exclusively to Christianity or presented as culturally universal. Describe the particular form being offered and its source.

Faithful attribution does not mean presenting religious claims as medical or scientific facts. Phrase metaphysical meanings in practitioner voice ("some practitioners understand...") and let visitors choose whether that framing fits them.

### Remove or materially reframe

- **Manifesting:** remove as a standalone practice. If retained, rename it **hopes, intentions, and possible next steps** and explicitly state that thoughts do not control illness, cure, prognosis, or other people's actions. The seed definition's combination of focus, visualization, action, and a desired future can imply causal control and self-blame in a setting where outcomes are often uncontrollable.
- **Affirmations:** retain only as user-authored supportive words or values, not compulsory positivity or promises (for example, avoid "I will heal" and "Everything happens for a reason"). Offer truthful alternatives such as "I can ask for company" and always permit skipping them.
- **Fasting:** remove from the self-guided practice catalog for pediatric hospice/palliative users. If the site mentions religious fasting, direct the family to their medical team and their own spiritual leader for an individualized adaptation; do not provide fasting instructions. Children with serious illness may have nutrition, hydration, medication, glucose, procedure, or symptom-management needs. Even pediatric anesthesia fasting is an individualized medical instruction that families must confirm with their team.[AAP/HealthyChildren, Preparing Your Child for Anesthesia](https://www.healthychildren.org/english/health-issues/conditions/treatments/pages/anesthesia-and-children-the-day-of-the-procedure.aspx)
- **Caregiving, mentoring, activism, and service:** do not frame these as duties or morally superior responses. A sick child and an exhausted family member must not be asked to prove spirituality through usefulness. Offer tiny, remote, assisted, symbolic, and receive-care options; make "rest" and "let someone care for me" equally valid.

## Safety constraints for retained practices

The resource must describe practices as optional supportive activities, not treatments, and must never suggest delaying or replacing care. NIH's pediatric guidance notes that safety evidence for many complementary approaches is limited and recommends discussing them with the child's healthcare providers; meditation, guided imagery, mindfulness, and yoga may be low-risk when appropriately used, but that does not make them universally suitable.[NCCIH, Children and the Use of Complementary Health Approaches](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/children-and-the-use-of-complementary-health-approaches)

- **Movement/yoga/labyrinth/sacred walks:** offer seated, lying-down, imagined, assisted, and no-movement versions. Ask the visitor to follow mobility, fall-risk, equipment, and clinical-team guidance. Never equate physical ability with spiritual participation.
- **Meditation, silence, guided imagery, breath, and repetition:** make eyes-open, shorter, externally focused, and stop-now alternatives available. Tell users to stop if distress, dizziness, pain, breathlessness, or unwanted memories increase. Meditation has reported negative effects for some participants, and its harms remain understudied.[NCCIH, Meditation and Mindfulness: Effectiveness and Safety](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/meditation-and-mindfulness-effectiveness-and-safety)
- **Aromatherapy/scents:** do not instruct users to ingest or apply essential oils, diffuse them in shared clinical spaces, or use them without clinical approval. Offer a no-scent alternative. Essential oils may produce allergy, irritation, coughing, drug concerns, or adverse personal associations; pediatric evidence is limited, and an NCI-reviewed pediatric transplant trial did not show benefit from bergamot inhalation.[NCI, Aromatherapy With Essential Oils](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/cam/hp/aromatherapy-pdq) Peppermint oil must not be applied to or inhaled near the face of infants or young children.[NCCIH, Peppermint Oil](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/peppermint-oil)
- **Animals:** require caregiver and clinical-setting permission, supervision appropriate to age and condition, hand hygiene, and a no-contact alternative. Children under five and immunocompromised people have higher risks from animal-borne germs.[CDC, People at Increased Risk for Illness from Animals](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-pets/risk-factors/index.html)
- **Objects, beads, art materials, charms, candles, and shrine items:** account for choking, ingestion, infection-control, line/tube entanglement, fire, allergy, and sharp-object risks. Offer digital, imagined, large-piece, and caregiver-assisted versions. Do not recommend lit flame in a general-purpose activity.
- **Service and creativity:** every exercise needs low-energy and stop-anytime variants. Avoid time targets and completion pressure.

## Release requirements implied by this audit

Before any practice is promoted from beta to production, its review record should confirm:

1. chaplain approval for spiritual-care fit and non-proselytizing language;
2. tradition review for any named or borrowed religious practice;
3. a pediatric safety review for any activity involving food/fluid restriction, ingestion, topical products, scent, breath manipulation, movement, animals, heat/flame, or small/sharp objects;
4. a plain-language, developmentally appropriate version plus relevant access adaptations;
5. an explicit opt-out, stop instruction, and nonreligious alternative where applicable;
6. no cure, symptom-relief, divine-favor, positive-thinking, or outcome guarantee; and
7. a source/provenance field that distinguishes tradition-owned descriptions, clinical safety sources, and the site's own editorial organization.

## Bottom line

The seed is suitable as inspiration and as an inventory of possibilities, not as publishable copy. Preserve the four pathway names only as porous navigation. Correct the terminology, reorganize duplicates, substantially qualify or remove the higher-risk practices, and rebuild each practice as a reviewed content record with provenance, tradition context, safety adaptations, and genuine permission to decline.
